# Assembly, visualization and documentation

Use Blender when rendering or assembly visualization benefits the deliverable. Import actual released engineering geometry, preserving part IDs, revisions, dimensions, positions and coordinate systems. A lookalike cannot validate the assembly. Check known dimensions after import, save/reopen the scene and inspect a rendered image.

Produce only the requested/useful subset: product views, labelled exploded view, numbered assembly steps, wiring/service illustrations, manual, animation. A complete demonstrator may need all of them; a concept comparison may need one clear section drawing. If an animation is requested without a duration, 30–60 seconds is a starting suggestion, not a requirement. Follow any applicable host animation workflow.

Check sequence, fit, motion clearances, tool access, wiring and part-replacement access against released files. Identify inaccessible operations or flexible parts the visualization does not model. Do not invent tightening torques or maintenance intervals. Label rendered motion as visualization, separate from kinematic/contact simulation and physical assembly testing. Keep editable source scenes alongside exports and record geometry provenance.
