# Electronics

Use KiCad or the established project tool when a schematic/PCB is actually needed. A purchased module or wiring diagram can be the smaller correct deliverable; do not force a custom PCB. A board-shaped CAD object is not a PCB design.

For a custom board, deliver the native project, schematic, routed board, bill of materials and calculations appropriate to the task. Use actual component identities, compatible symbols/footprints, and primary datasheets. Verify the full supply range, current and transient cases, connector and protection limits, trace sizing and thermal dissipation. Include power protection, switching, controls, voltage/temperature monitoring, and firmware only where function or risk requires them. Do not import a previous product's circuit architecture.

Unknown electrical values remain assumptions; model a plausible justified range and derive a design rule or measurement need. Distinguish module specifications, chip specifications and unverified clone boards.

Agree board outline, mounting holes, keep-outs, component heights, connectors, wire routing and service access with mechanical CAD. Track both revisions and coordinate units/axes. Run ERC and DRC, inspect their reports, resolve faults and document every waiver. Save/reopen native files and inspect exported results. Rule-check success does not establish hardware function, reliability, compliance or safe energization. Compile firmware only if requested/needed; report compilation separately from flashing and bench/game tests.

Do not purchase, submit production files, flash, or power hardware without authorization for that action.
